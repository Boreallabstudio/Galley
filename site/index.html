Galley

A galley is a long column of set type, made to be read before the pages are cut.

How it reads Keys Make it yours Design note

The transcript is the player.

Galley is a Video.js 10 skin made for people who watch with subtitles on. Words ink in as they are spoken, and choosing any word plays from there.

Space play or pause ← → five seconds [ ] a line at a time C captions F fullscreen / find a word

I How it reads

The seek bar is a sentence

Hover or drag the rule beneath the page and the card above it shows the words spoken at that moment, not a thumbnail, while the page turns to the same line and marks it. You read your way to where you want to be.

Every word has an address

Point at a word and its time appears in the gutter. Choose it and playback jumps there. The page keeps following the speaker, until you scroll away to read ahead. A small button brings you back.

Notes live in the margin

A second text track holds notes. Each one is numbered beside the words it belongs to and darkens while it applies, as in a printed edition. Chapters become numbered section heads, and are set on the seek rule as section marks.

III Make it yours

Galley answers to the same custom properties as the packaged Video.js skins, and adds one of its own. 

<video-player>
  <media-container class="media-skin gl" data-theme="galley" style="
      --media-accent-color: #2346c8;   /* the pencil: any colour you like */
      --media-font-family: 'Newsreader', Georgia, serif;
      --media-border-radius: 6px;
      --gl-plate-ratio: 16 / 9;        /* the picture's shape */
  ">
    <video src="talk.mp4" playsinline>
      <track kind="captions"  srclang="en" label="English" src="talk.vtt" default>
      <track kind="chapters"  srclang="en" label="English" src="chapters.vtt">
      <track kind="metadata"  label="galley" src="notes.vtt">
    </video>
    …
  </media-container>
</video-player>

Captions can carry word timing as WebVTT inline timestamps, such as <00:01:02.500>. Without them, words are spread across each caption by length. A caption whose identifier begins with ¶ starts a new paragraph.

IV Design note

Most players put the picture first and the words behind a button. For talks, lectures, interviews and speeches, the words are what people came for. Galley reverses the order: the text is the page, the picture is its plate, and the controls are set the way a book sets a folio, in small capitals with no icons.

Colour carries one meaning. Grey is unread, ink is read, and the single accent, the blue pencil editors once marked copy with, marks where you are: the rule under the current line, the playhead on the seek bar, the reference marks, and every focus ring. Nothing is signalled by colour alone: the active word also gains an underline, and the line being read is marked as current for screen readers.

Motion is limited to ink arriving and the page following the speaker. With prefers-reduced-motion the ink changes without fading and the page jumps rather than glides. Increased contrast darkens the grey, and forced colours map the ink, rule and controls to system colours.

It is built from Video.js 10 components with seven small custom elements: the text, the line over the picture, the chapter title, the seek card, the section marks on the rule, the picture itself and the contents page. They read the media's own <track> elements and state from the player, so the skin works with any source Video.js plays.

The demonstration plays President John F. Kennedy’s address at Rice University, Houston, 12 September 1962, shortened to the speech itself. Film from the Internet Archive, where it is marked as public domain. Captions, word timing, chapters and notes were prepared for this skin.
